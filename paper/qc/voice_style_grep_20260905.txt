== measured wiring ==
paper/flycraft_bibm_draft.tex:22:A connectome is a map of connections between nerve cells. This study investigates whether a fruit-fly nerve-cord model can use these measured connections to move a different six-legged body. The model uses MaleCNS v1.0 data and contains 11,000 model nodes with 485,074 signed connections. A fixed conversion sends 260 motor signals to the 18 joints of a simulated hexapod. Neither the nerve-cord connections nor this conversion undergo training. The measured wiring produced 0.224 m of forward displacement over 8 s, compared with an average of $-0.012$ m for 12 controls with shuffled connection weights. Each control matched the variation in the measured model's joint targets. The movement advantage remained across six changes to the body settings. However, the nerve-cord output did not pass the expected leg-timing test for alternating-tripod coordination. These results show that the measured wiring affects movement through this interface, while coordinated walking remains unresolved. Ongoing work trains the body interface while keeping the nerve-cord connections fixed.

== fixed motor ==
paper/flycraft_bibm_draft.tex:86:The simulated body is a PhantomX-style hexapod with three joints per leg. MuJoCo computes its movement and interaction with the ground~\cite{todorov2012mujoco}. The fixed motor conversion combines opposing motor signals into a target angle for each joint. It uses fly coxa signals for the proximal robot joint, combines trochanter/femur signals for the middle joint, and combines available tibia/tarsus signals for the outer joint. These names refer to successive parts of the fly leg. Missing motor groups keep their joint at its home angle.

Only the encoder, decoder, and training value model learn; the nerve-cord weights stay fixed. The decoder replaces the earlier fixed motor conversion. Target information comes directly from the simulation, so this stage does not reproduce fly vision.
